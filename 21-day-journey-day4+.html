I equip you, though you do not know me, that people may know, from the rising of the sun and from the west, that there is none besides me; I am the Lord, and there is no other.

Isaiah 45:1-7

What’s in a name?

There’s a reason we wear name tags at networking events, or why we had those colorful signs taped to our desks in elementary school: People love to hear their own name, and it’s an essential first step in making connections. 

Today we’re talking about the next step after noticing: naming. To name someone means we start to get to know them as an individual, to learn the things that make them different from everybody else. (Is it their coffee order? The way they laugh? Their interest in sci-fi movies?) 

It may sound so simple, but you can’t move beyond casual surface interactions without first knowing someone by name. It’s comforting and familiar, and makes them feel seen and known.

Thinking about naming

  • Have you experienced an interaction where someone misunderstood who you are until they knew you better? How did that feel?
  • Is there someone in your life that you interact with regularly but don’t know well? How can you take a step toward knowing them better?

Try This

Use your same note from yesterday and start to collect names of people you meet, with a word or two of context. (It might feel strange at first, but it’ll help your busy brain retain names among all the things you have to think about.) Choose one person to pray about each day.

Pray

As I learn to see those around me, help me get to know someone new by name.
